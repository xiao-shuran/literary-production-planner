# Presentation And Rehearsal Guide

Use this guide when the user needs to turn a literary adaptation into a stage, video, audio, or competition presentation. Apply only the parts relevant to the chosen medium.

## Stage And Classroom Presentation

For each scene, specify entrances, position changes, key action, exits, or transitions. Convert large-scale, difficult-to-stage events into rehearsal-ready focal actions, sound, light, projection, or props instead of listing unachievable effects. Props must serve the plot and suit the venue constraints.

## Short Video And Shot Plans

Write every segment as a shootable unit: framing or viewpoint, subject action, ambient sound/music, dialogue or narration, transition, and estimated duration. Do not overload a single shot with conflicting audiovisual requirements. Let visuals carry emotion and information where possible, reducing repeated narration.

## Audio And Recital

Clarify who speaks, where a sound comes from, and how pauses and effects help listeners construct space. In audio-only work, role distinction and information delivery need greater clarity than on stage, but do not narrate every action.

## Coherence Check

- Can the scene design be completed within the allotted duration, cast size, and space?
- Do music, sound effects, projection, costumes, and props have a distinct plot or emotional purpose?
- Does every transition have an executable cue and responsible person?
- Do performance, narration, visuals, and technical cues compete for attention at the same moment? If so, keep the most necessary one.
