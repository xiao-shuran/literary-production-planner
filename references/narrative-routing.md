# Narrative Routing Guide

Read this guide before designing any new literary adaptation. Its purpose is to prevent a fixed planning template from flattening different kinds of literature into the same audiovisual or stage shape.

## The Routing Principle

Classify the work by the **thing that would be lost first if the adaptation were careless**, not merely by the label on its cover. A novel can be primarily atmospheric; an essay can be built as a mystery; a historical story can be carried by one relationship.

Choose one primary narrative engine and, when needed, one secondary engine. Use the primary engine to decide scene architecture, narration, performance, sound, visual motifs, rhythm, and the weight given to each planning heading.

## Source-Type Routes

### Plot-Driven Fiction Or Adventure

- Protect causal clarity, active choices, escalating obstacles, reversals, and the resolution.
- Prefer sequences with visible objectives and consequences. Use narration only to bridge information that cannot be dramatised economically.
- Rhythm is often driven by question, obstacle, attempt, setback, and change of plan.
- Visual motifs should sharpen stakes or orientation, not delay events.
- Failure signal: the adaptation becomes a list of episodes or a narrator explains every turn before it happens.

### Relationship-Driven Fiction Or Drama

- Protect changing distances between people: power, trust, care, jealousy, misunderstanding, dependence, or farewell.
- Build scenes around playable exchanges and behavior. Use close framing, blocking, interruption, silence, and repeated gestures as appropriate.
- Narration should be sparse unless the original's narrator voice is indispensable.
- Rhythm comes from escalation, withholding, rupture, repair, or an unresolved residue rather than plot quantity.
- Failure signal: the plan turns nuanced relationships into exposition or has characters state feelings they could show through action.

### Interior Or Psychological Fiction

- Protect the subjective shift: what the protagonist notices, avoids, remembers, misreads, or finally understands.
- A hybrid of action, subjective image, sound, restrained narration, diary/letter fragments, or voice-over may be appropriate, but each device needs a distinct job.
- Use visual and sound motifs to externalize inner pressure without translating every thought literally.
- Rhythm may move through association and repetition-with-variation rather than large events.
- Failure signal: explanatory narration replaces experience, or arbitrary dreamlike images substitute for a coherent inner point of view.

### Essay, Lyric Prose, Or Landscape Writing

- Protect voice, perception, imagery, conceptual movement, and the relation between observer and world; do not invent a conventional protagonist and conflict just to fill a film template.
- Consider narrated image poem, staged reading, essay film, sensory montage, or a lightly framed observer journey.
- Let images make arguments with juxtaposition. Use silence and sound texture as structural material.
- Motifs often develop by seasonal, spatial, or perceptual change rather than plot reversal.
- Failure signal: generic travel montage, decorative imagery unrelated to the prose, or excessive visual literalism that flattens metaphor.

### Poetry, Lyrics, Or Highly Compressed Symbolic Writing

- Protect rhythm, sonic texture, ambiguity, lineation or repetition patterns, images, and the poem's emotional movement.
- Do not “explain the poem” through narration. Choose a performance score, visual poem, movement piece, voice collage, or minimal dramatic frame according to the text.
- Use a very limited image vocabulary; repetition with changed context is usually stronger than many illustrations.
- Treat pauses, breath, silence, and sound as structural, not filler.
- Failure signal: one visual per line, sentimental background music, or a forced plot that erases the poem's open space.

### Letters, Diaries, Testimony, Or Memoir

- Protect first-person voice, time distance, selectivity of memory, and the ethical weight of the speaker's account.
- Consider direct address, recorded voice, staged reading, material-object close-ups, archive-like framing, or dialogue between past and present selves.
- Clearly distinguish what is remembered, quoted, reconstructed, or newly staged. Do not fabricate evidence-like details.
- Rhythm can follow acts of disclosure: concealment, fragment, contradiction, recognition, and release.
- Failure signal: treating testimony as generic fiction or presenting invented scenes as documented fact.

### Historical, Documentary, Or Social-Realist Material

- Protect factual accuracy, provenance, causality, affected people's dignity, and the distinction between evidence and dramatization.
- Decide whether the form is documentary montage, testimony, researched drama, newsroom structure, or a single-character lens; name the source basis in the plan when relevant.
- Visual motifs can organize history, but must not aestheticize suffering or replace context.
- Rhythm comes from evidence, human consequence, changing understanding, and responsibly framed stakes.
- Failure signal: unsupported claims, invented quotations, sensational reenactment, or turning real harm into visual decoration.

### Mystery, Suspense, Or Revelation-Driven Work

- Protect what the audience knows, suspects, and learns at each stage. Track information separately from character emotion.
- Give each scene a question, clue, misdirection, confirmation, or consequence. Use restricted framing and sound to manage access to information.
- Narration should not spoil the question or reveal a clue before the viewer can encounter it.
- Rhythm alternates tension, partial release, new uncertainty, and revelation.
- Failure signal: vague atmosphere without a readable question, or a final explanation that has not been prepared by earlier information.

### Myth, Fable, Allegory, Or Folktale

- Protect the governing image, ritual logic, moral tension, transformation, and oral/storytelling quality where present.
- A narrator, chorus, stylized staging, recurring objects, or rule-based visual design may be more faithful than naturalistic realism.
- State the world's rules early enough for symbolic choices to read.
- Rhythm is often ceremonial or cyclical, with controlled repetition and a meaningful break in the pattern.
- Failure signal: over-literal realism that drains symbolic force, or unexplained symbolism that reads as random.

### Existing Stage Drama

- Protect playable conflict, scene objectives, subtext, entrances/exits, and the theatrical relationship with the audience.
- Do not automatically convert it to voice-over-heavy video. Decide whether it should remain staged, become a filmed stage work, or be cinematically adapted for a clear reason.
- Preserve dialogue only where it remains playable; trim or reframe rather than making actors recite blocks of prose.
- Failure signal: film coverage that removes the play's spatial tension, or a stage plan that ignores its rhythm of entrances, silence, and audience attention.

## Cross-Type Decision Rules

### Choose Narration By Necessity

Use narration when it preserves a source voice, makes a time or viewpoint leap economical, creates necessary distance, or carries a conceptual movement that image and dialogue cannot. Do not use it merely because the original was written prose.

### Choose The Primary Form By Strength

The form should amplify the work's strongest native resource:

| Strongest resource | Likely primary form |
| --- | --- |
| Conflict in present action | Dialogue scene / dramatic sequence |
| Distinct inner voice | Hybrid subjective sequence / voiced performance |
| Perception and imagery | Visual poem / essay film / sensory montage |
| Evidence and recollection | Testimony, archival, or documentary-style structure |
| Information gap and reveal | Suspense sequence |
| Ritual and symbol | Stylized stage piece / chorus / rule-based visual system |

These are routes, not mandatory labels. Choose another form only when you can state why it better protects the source's audience contract.

### Adapt The Framework, Not The Source To The Framework

When the user supplies a fixed framework, translate its headings into source-specific questions:

| Template heading | Source-specific question |
| --- | --- |
| Creative core | What is this work's irreplaceable engine and audience contract? |
| Form and style | Why is this medium and primary form right for this source? |
| Structure | What are this source's meaningful units: scenes, images, stanzas, disclosures, clues, or rituals? |
| Narration | What can only the voice carry, and what must remain visible or playable? |
| Visual design | Which motif system is native to the work rather than borrowed from a generic film look? |
| Storyboard / staging | What does each shot or stage image newly accomplish? |
| Quality review | What would count as betrayal, flattening, confusion, or loss for this type of source? |

## Route Check Before Drafting

Write a five-line internal decision record before planning:

```text
Source form:
Primary / secondary narrative engine:
Audience contract:
Primary adaptation form and why:
What this adaptation must not become:
```

If the final line describes a generic previous project, a generic “cinematic” style, or a template requirement rather than a source-specific failure, revisit the diagnosis.
