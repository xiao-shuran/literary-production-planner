# Final Quality Review Guide

Use this guide before delivering an audiovisual plan, and when the user supplies a completed cut, rough cut, or exported file for review. Review observable work rather than giving generic praise.

## Review Process

1. Watch or read the work once without pausing. Record the intended story, emotional turning point, dominant motif, and any moment of confusion or disengagement.
2. Review a second time against the checks below. Attach each finding to a timecode, scene, shot, or planning section when available.
3. Separate blocking failures from refinements. Fix failures that prevent comprehension, undermine the source, break the requested form, or make the piece technically unusable before polishing style.
4. End with a short release decision: `ready`, `ready after targeted fixes`, or `not ready`. State the specific evidence for that decision.

## Content And Adaptation

- The audience can follow who wants what, what changes, and why the ending matters.
- The adaptation preserves the source's required relationships, turns, theme, and ending, or clearly labels intentional changes.
- Scenes have a cause-and-effect progression. Exposition, narration, and dialogue do not carry the same information three times.
- The decisive emotional moment is dramatized through action, performance, image, or sound rather than only explained afterward.

## Motif And Rhythm

- The motif system is concrete, appears with variation, and changes meaning by the end.
- Each return of a motif is legible to the audience without calling excessive attention to itself.
- The emotional curve has discernible setup, development, peak, and residue. The peak has been protected from competing dialogue, music, explanatory narration, or rapid cutting.
- Pacing includes contrast: scenes do not all have the same duration, shot scale, energy, or music level without reason.
- Transitions tell the audience whether time, place, or emotional state has changed.

## Shot And Staging Value

- Every retained shot or stage image has a distinct primary value: story, character, relationship, orientation, motif, rhythm, or atmosphere.
- Repetitive inserts, reaction shots, establishing shots, montage images, and slow motion have been cut or varied when they add no new meaning.
- Framing, camera movement, blocking, and duration match the shot's value. A close-up is earned by a revelation; a wide shot earns spatial or emotional distance.
- The shot list can be executed with the actual cast, locations, time, equipment, and budget.

## Performance, Narration, And Sound

- Dialogue sounds like speech and creates a change; actors are not reciting prose at each other.
- Narration is needed, concise, and complementary to the visible action. If it is removed, only essential context should be lost.
- Acting direction has playable behavior, not only abstract emotions such as “be sad” or “be moved.”
- Dialogue is intelligible. Narration, music, ambient sound, and effects have intentional levels and do not compete for attention.
- Silence and room tone are used deliberately at turns, rather than being filled with continuous music.

## Visual And Technical Finish

- Exposure, white balance, color, focus, frame rate, and aspect ratio are consistent enough that accidental technical shifts do not distract.
- Essential faces, actions, text, and props are visible long enough to be understood.
- Cuts respect screen direction, eyelines, action continuity, and the stated passage of time unless a break is intentional and readable.
- Captions, titles, credits, and on-screen text are accurate, readable, timed long enough, and do not cover critical visual information.
- The final runtime, export format, resolution, audio mix, and required deliverables match the submission brief.

## Findings Format

When reporting a review, list findings in this order:

1. `Blocking`: a failure that makes the piece unclear, unperformable, technically unusable, or contrary to the brief.
2. `Important`: a material issue with emotion, pacing, motif, shot value, performance, or sound that should be corrected before release.
3. `Polish`: a non-blocking improvement that would strengthen finish or clarity.

Each finding must state the location, the observed issue, why it matters to the audience, and a concrete revision. Do not manufacture findings simply to make the review look thorough.
